P-01.04 · Icosahedron¶

Icosahedron · Field: :math:`mathbb{Q}(phi)` · Symmetry: :math:`I_h` (order 120) · Convex · Euclid Elements XIII.16

To construct a regular icosahedron inside a given sphere and to prove that the side of the icosahedron is an irrational straight line called minor.

—Euclid of Alexandria, Elements Book XIII, Proposition 16 (G-Index: G.597)

The Regular Icosahedron is the highest-order deltahedral Platonic solid, possessing 20 congruent equilateral triangular faces, 12 vertices, and 30 edges (\(\{3, 5\}\)). In Plato’s Timaeus (56a–56b), the icosahedron is assigned to the element Water: with the greatest number of faces, the most obtuse dihedral angles, and the closest approximation to a rolling sphere, it is the most mobile, fluid, and least sharp of all triangular polyhedra.

Face Cycles & Normal Orientations¶

The twenty equilateral triangular faces are oriented with counter-clockwise outward normal cycles:

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} F_0 &= (0, 2, 8), & F_1 &= (0, 10, 2), & F_2 &= (0, 4, 6), & F_3 &= (0, 8, 4), & F_4 &= (0, 6, 10) \\ F_5 &= (1, 9, 3), & F_6 &= (1, 3, 11), & F_7 &= (1, 6, 4), & F_8 &= (1, 4, 9), & F_9 &= (1, 11, 6) \\ F_{10} &= (2, 7, 5), & F_{11} &= (2, 5, 8), & F_{12} &= (2, 10, 7), & F_{13} &= (3, 5, 7), & F_{14} &= (3, 9, 5) \\ F_{15} &= (3, 7, 11), & F_{16} &= (4, 8, 9), & F_{17} &= (5, 9, 8), & F_{18} &= (6, 11, 10), & F_{19} &= (7, 10, 11) \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Dihedral Spread Derivation¶

The cosine of the interior dihedral angle between two adjacent faces is:

\[\cos\theta = -\frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}\]

Squaring this value produces \(\cos^2\theta = \frac{5}{9}\). In Rational Trigonometry, the dihedral spread is:

\[s = 1 - \cos^2\theta = 1 - \frac{5}{9} = \frac{4}{9}\]

Important

Pure Rational Dihedral Spread: Although expressing individual vertex coordinates and dihedral angles requires the quadratic golden field \(\mathbb{Q}(\phi)\), the planar dihedral spread between adjacent faces is the exact rational fraction 4/9!
