P-02 · Archimedean & Abscisa Solids¶

Uniform Corner Truncations, Edge-Midpoint Rectifications, and Leonardo’s Vigintisex Basium

Archimedes discovered thirteen solids bounded by regular polygons of more than one kind, every vertex being surrounded in the same manner by the same figures.

—Pappus of Alexandria, Synagoge (Collection), Book V

Abscisa corpora sunt quae ex regularibus nascendo per abscisionem angulorum solidorum fiunt… inter quae eminet vigintisex basium planum solidum et vacuum.

The truncated bodies are those which originate from the regular solids through the cutting away of their solid angles… among which the twenty-six base body, solid and hollow, stands supreme.

—Luca Pacioli, De Divina Proportione, Cap. LXI (Venice, 1509)

The Archimedean Canon in GEOMETOR¶

While the five Platonic solids are strictly isohedral (face-transitive) and isogonal (vertex-transitive) with a single regular polygon type, the Archimedean Solids are semi-regular convex polyhedra: their faces are regular polygons of two or more distinct types, yet every vertex is symmetrically equivalent under the action of the symmetry group (isogonal).

In the GEOMETOR formulation, eight canonical Archimedean and Abscisa polyhedra are modeled with 100% exact symbolic coordinates across three algebraic fields:

  1. The Rational Field \(\mathbb{Q}\): The Truncated Tetrahedron, Cuboctahedron, and Truncated Octahedron.

  2. The Quadratic Extension \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\): The Truncated Hexahedron and Leonardo’s Rhombicuboctahedron (Vigintisex Basium).

  3. The Golden Algebraic Field \(\mathbb{Q}(\phi)\): The Icosidodecahedron, Truncated Icosahedron (Fullerene Buckyball), and Truncated Dodecahedron.

Constructive Operators: Truncation and Rectification¶

Every Archimedean solid in this family is generated from a primary Platonic solid by an exact algebraic operator implemented in geometor.polyhedra.operators:

Corner Truncation: truncate(solid, t)¶

Cutting off each vertex of a solid along planes perpendicular to the vertex radius at an exact fractional edge depth \(t \in (0, 1/2)\) replaces each \(n\)-valent vertex with a regular \(n\)-gon and transforms each original \(k\)-gon into a \(2k\)-gon:

  • Rational Truncation (\(t = 1/3\)): Cutting the regular tetrahedron at one-third edge length yields 4 triangles and 4 regular hexagons (Truncated Tetrahedron). Cutting the regular octahedron at \(t = 1/3\) yields 6 squares and 8 regular hexagons (Truncated Octahedron, the Kelvin space-filling permutohedron).

  • Octagonal Truncation (\(t = 1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)): Cutting the cube at depth \(t = 1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\) yields 8 triangles and 6 regular octagons (Truncated Hexahedron).

  • Fullerene Truncation (\(t = 1/3\)): Cutting the regular icosahedron at one-third edge length yields 12 regular pentagons and 20 regular hexagons (Truncated Icosahedron, the canonical \(C_{60}\) soccer ball / buckyball).

  • Golden Decagonal Truncation (\(t = \frac{3 - \phi}{5}\)): Cutting the regular dodecahedron at exact depth \(t = \frac{3 - \phi}{5} = \frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{10} \in \mathbb{Q}(\phi)\) yields 20 triangles and 12 regular decagons (Truncated Dodecahedron).

Edge-Midpoint Rectification: rectify(solid)¶

When the truncation depth reaches the exact midpoint of every edge (\(t = 1/2\)), the original faces contract into their centroids and vertices merge:

  • Cuboctahedron (Exahedron Abscisum): Rectifying either the cube or the octahedron produces the unique 14-faceted body with 12 vertices, 24 edges of uniform quadrance 2, and 8 triangular + 6 square faces.

  • Icosidodecahedron (Dodecahedron Abscisum): Rectifying either the dodecahedron or the icosahedron produces the unique 32-faceted golden ratio body with 30 vertices, 60 edges, and 20 triangular + 12 pentagonal faces.

Leonardo da Vinci’s 26-Base Body: Vigintisex Basium¶

In Luca Pacioli’s De Divina Proportione (Cap. LXI, Tabulae XXXV–XXXVI), Leonardo da Vinci celebrated the Rhombicuboctahedron under the name Vigintisex Basium (the 26-Base Body). Bounded by 18 squares (6 axial and 12 chamfer facets) and 8 equilateral octant triangles, it is modeled over \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\) with 24 vertices and 48 edges of uniform quadrance 4.

The Eight Archimedean Monograph Folios¶

P-02.01 · Truncated Tetrahedron

The Truncated Tetrahedron (Tetrahedron Abscisum) is the simplest Archimedean semi-regular polyhedron, formed by cutting off the four corners of a regular tetrahedron at one-third of its edge length (\(t = 1/3\)). Bounded by 4 equilateral triangles and 4 regular hexagons with 12 vertices and 18 edges, every vertex is surrounded identically by one triangle and two hexagons (\(3 \cdot 6^2\)).
02-archimedean archimedean truncation tetrahedron pacioli semi-regular

P-02.02 · Cuboctahedron

The Cuboctahedron (Exahedron Abscisum) is the canonical quasi-regular Archimedean solid formed by edge-midpoint rectification (rectify) of either the cube or the regular octahedron. Bounded by 8 equilateral triangles and 6 squares with 12 vertices and 24 edges, every vertex is surrounded by alternating triangles and squares (\((3 \cdot 4)^2\)).
02-archimedean archimedean rectification cube octahedron pacioli quasi-regular

P-02.03 · Truncated Hexahedron

The Truncated Hexahedron (Truncated Cube) is an Archimedean semi-regular solid formed by truncating the eight corners of a cube at edge depth \(t = 1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\). Bounded by 8 equilateral triangles and 6 regular octagons with 24 vertices and 36 edges, every vertex is surrounded identically by one triangle and two octagons (\(3 \cdot 8^2\)).
02-archimedean archimedean truncation cube octagon sqrt2

P-02.04 · Truncated Octahedron

The Truncated Octahedron (Octahedron Abscisum, Lord Kelvin’s ideal foam cell, and the order-4 permutohedron) is the unique space-filling Archimedean solid formed by truncating the six corners of a regular octahedron at one-third edge depth (\(t = 1/3\)). Bounded by 6 squares and 8 regular hexagons with 24 vertices and 36 edges, every vertex is surrounded by one square and two hexagons (\(4 \cdot 6^2\)).
02-archimedean archimedean truncation octahedron space-filling permutohedron pacioli

P-02.05 · Rhombicuboctahedron

The Rhombicuboctahedron (Vigintisex Basium, the 26-Base Body) is the celebrated centerpiece of Renaissance stereometry depicted by Leonardo da Vinci. Bounded by 18 squares (6 axial and 12 chamfer facets) and 8 equilateral octant triangles with 24 vertices and 48 edges, every vertex is surrounded by three squares and one triangle (\(3 \cdot 4^3\)).
02-archimedean archimedean rhombicuboctahedron pacioli leonardo sqrt2 vigintisex-basium

P-02.06 · Icosidodecahedron

The Icosidodecahedron (Dodecahedron Abscisum) is the canonical quasi-regular golden ratio solid formed by edge-midpoint rectification (rectify) of either the regular dodecahedron or regular icosahedron. Bounded by 20 equilateral triangles and 12 regular pentagons with 30 vertices and 60 edges, every vertex is surrounded by alternating triangles and pentagons (\((3 \cdot 5)^2\)).
02-archimedean archimedean rectification dodecahedron icosahedron phi golden-ratio pacioli

P-02.07 · Truncated Icosahedron

The Truncated Icosahedron (Icosahedron Abscisum, the canonical Archimedean fullerene / Buckminsterfullerene \(C_{60}\)) is formed by truncating the twelve vertices of a regular icosahedron at one-third edge depth (\(t = 1/3\)). Bounded by 12 regular pentagons and 20 regular hexagons with 60 vertices and 90 edges, every vertex is surrounded identically by one pentagon and two hexagons (\(5 \cdot 6^2\)).
02-archimedean archimedean truncation icosahedron fullerene buckyball phi pacioli

P-02.08 · Truncated Dodecahedron

The Truncated Dodecahedron (Dodecahedron Abscisum Decagonale) is an Archimedean semi-regular solid formed by truncating the twenty vertices of a regular dodecahedron at the exact golden edge depth \(t = \frac{3 - \phi}{5} = \frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{10} \in \mathbb{Q}(\phi)\). Bounded by 20 equilateral triangles and 12 regular decagons with 60 vertices and 90 edges, every vertex is surrounded identically by one triangle and two decagons (\(3 \cdot 10^2\)).
