P-01.01 · Tetrahedron

The Regular Tetrahedron is the primary regular convex polyhedron in three-dimensional Euclidean space. Bounded by 4 equilateral triangular faces with 4 vertices and 6 edges, each vertex is shared by 3 triangles (\(\{3, 3\}\)). In Plato’s Timaeus (56b), the tetrahedron is assigned to the element Fire: having the fewest faces, the sharpest solid angles, and the greatest piercing power, it is the lightest, keenest, and most mobile of all elementary bodies.

P-02.01 · Truncated Tetrahedron

The Truncated Tetrahedron (Tetrahedron Abscisum) is the simplest Archimedean semi-regular polyhedron, formed by cutting off the four corners of a regular tetrahedron at one-third of its edge length (\(t = 1/3\)). Bounded by 4 equilateral triangles and 4 regular hexagons with 12 vertices and 18 edges, every vertex is surrounded identically by one triangle and two hexagons (\(3 \cdot 6^2\)).

P-04.01 · Tetrahedron Elevatum

A stellated polyhedron formed by erecting equilateral triangular pyramids on the four faces of a tetrahedron.

Tabulae I–II: Tetrahedron Planum Solidum et Vacuum (1509)

To illustrate Luca Pacioli’s De Divina Proportione (1509), Leonardo da Vinci constructed the regular tetrahedron \(\{3, 3\}\) in two foundational aspects: Tabula I (Folio 0187) as an opaque solid body with directional shading (Planum Solidum), and Tabula II (Folio 0189) as an open mitered wooden lattice (Planum Vacuum). Through the Vacuate Manifold Theorem, hollowing the four triangular faces produces a closed orientable 2-manifold of topological genus \(g = F - 1 = 3\).

George Odom’s Golden Construction in the Tetrahedron (1983)

In 1983, the American artist and geometer George Odom discovered that connecting the midpoints \(M_1\) and \(M_2\) of two sides of an equilateral triangular face of a regular tetrahedron and extending the segment to intersect the face’s circumcircle at \(G_2\) divides the chord in the divine proportion: \(Q(M_1, M_2) / Q(M_2, G_2) = \phi^2\), proving that \(\phi\) is an organic invariant of tetrahedral geometry.
