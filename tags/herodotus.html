The 1597 Kepler Right Triangle & Golden Surface Harmony

In October 1597, Johannes Kepler synthesized the Pythagorean Theorem (\(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\)) and the Divine Proportion (\(1 + \phi = \phi^2\)) into a singular right-angled triangle whose sides form a continuous geometric progression \((1, \sqrt{\phi}, \phi)\). When this triangle serves as the meridian cross-section of a square pyramid, the square on the vertical height equals the area of each triangular face (\(A_{\text{face}} = h^2\)), fulfilling Herodotus’s ancient architectural enunciation over the quartic number field \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\phi})\).
