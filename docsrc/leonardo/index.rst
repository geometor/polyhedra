Leonardo da Vinci's Polyhedral Plates (1509)
=============================================

*The Sixty Woodcut Plates of Luca Pacioli's De Divina Proportione*

.. epigraph::

   *...per mano del nostro Lionardo da Vinci Fiorentino...*

   *...by the hand of our own Leonardo da Vinci the Florentine...*

   — :person:`Luca Pacioli`, *De Divina Proportione*, Cap. I (Venice, 1509)

Historical Context
------------------

In 1496, the Franciscan friar and mathematician **Luca Pacioli** arrived at the Milanese court of Duke Ludovico Sforza (*Il Moro*). There he formed a close collaboration with **Leonardo da Vinci**, teaching Leonardo Euclidean proportion and perspective geometry.

In return, Leonardo created sixty masterly drawings of polyhedra to illustrate Pacioli's treatise *De Divina Proportione* (completed in manuscript in 1498 and published at Venice by Paganino de' Paganini in 1509).

Taxonomy of Variations
----------------------

For each regular solid, Leonardo designed four principal architectural variations:

1. **Solidum** (*Planum Solidum*): A solid body with opaque planar facets, rendered with woodcut hatching to indicate illumination.
2. **Vacuum** (*Planum Vacuum*): A hollow, open-lattice wooden framework revealing internal geometry, opposite faces, and perspective depth.
3. **Abscisum** (*Truncatum*): The Archimedean truncated solid, slicing off vertices to create new regular facets.
4. **Elevatum** (*Stellatum*): The stellated solid, erecting regular pyramids on each polygonal face.
